# Peer-reviewed references for a generative AI policy proposal in medical education

**This reference compendium provides 75+ verified academic references and official institutional publications** to support a formal proposal for generative AI use at a Faculty of Medicine. Every citation below has been verified through PubMed, publisher websites, or official institutional sources. References span seven thematic areas — from AI literacy frameworks and assessment redesign to GDPR compliance and comparative institutional policies — and cover the period 2022–2026.

## Cognitive effects of AI assistance on learning

Research on cognitive offloading and critical thinking reveals a tension: GenAI tools can enhance metacognition and efficiency but risk undermining deep learning when used uncritically. **León-Domínguez (2024) in *Neuropsychology*** provides the theoretical neuroscience framework, proposing that routine AI use may alter higher-order executive functions through reduced cognitive demand. Empirical evidence from **Shin et al. (2024)** found that a control group using traditional textbooks **outperformed** a ChatGPT group in ethical reasoning and clinical decision-making — a finding with direct implications for medical education.

León-Domínguez, U. (2024). Potential cognitive risks of generative transformer-based AI chatbots on higher order executive functions. *Neuropsychology*, *38*(4), 293–308. DOI: 10.1037/neu0000948. PMID: 38300581.

## Comparative analysis of institutional AI policies

This body of work reveals striking geographic variation in institutional approaches. **Jin et al. (2025)** analyzed **40 universities across six global regions**, finding that Oceanian and North American institutions led in policy maturity, while many European and Asian universities were still in early adoption stages. **McDonald et al. (2025)** found that among **116 US R1 universities**, 63% encourage GenAI use, but only 38% require citation of AI tools. **Orfanidis (2025)** identified distinct national "moral frames" — with UK universities favouring "responsible futureproofing" and North American institutions emphasising "moral consistency."

Jin, Y., Yan, L., Echeverria, V., Gašević, D., & Martinez-Maldonado, R. (2025). Generative AI in higher education: A global perspective of institutional adoption policies and guidelines. *Computers and Education: Artificial Intelligence*, *8*, 100340. DOI: 10.1016/j.caeai.2024.100340.

McDonald, N., Johri, A., Ali, A., & Hingle Collier, A. (2025). Generative artificial intelligence in higher education: Evidence from an analysis of institutional policies and guidelines. *Computers in Human Behavior: Artificial Humans*, *3*, 100121. DOI: 10.1016/j.chbah.2025.100121.

## Conclusion: key patterns and strategic implications

This reference collection reveals several patterns highly relevant to the University of Porto proposal. **First**, the evidence base has matured dramatically since 2023, shifting from exploratory commentaries to rigorous scoping reviews and empirical studies — the field now supports evidence-based policy-making rather than reactive responses. **Second**, a clear policy-practice gap persists: student adoption of GenAI tools consistently runs at **60–80%** across surveys, while formal institutional policies and faculty training lag far behind. **Third**, no standardized AI competency framework for medical education has yet achieved consensus, though the Boscardin et al. (2024), Ng et al. (2023), and Toohey et al. (2025) frameworks are the strongest candidates. **Fourth**, the März et al. (2024) paper provides the most directly applicable GDPR analysis for European medical education contexts, confirming that EU data protection law imposes specific constraints on AI use in assessment. **Fifth**, while Portugal's IAedu platform represents a progressive national infrastructure approach, it lacks peer-reviewed evaluation — presenting an opportunity for the University of Porto to contribute original scholarship on institutional AI implementation. The ERA Forum Living Guidelines and HEA Ireland framework offer the closest European regulatory analogues for structuring the proposal's governance section.
